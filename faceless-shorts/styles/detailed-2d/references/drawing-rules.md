# Primary drawing context

Evidence: 120 overview frames across ten videos, plus 48 frames from six selected four-second windows. This is sampled visual inspection, not full playback or audio analysis. Original project rigs are unavailable; the proposed joint implementation is a reconstruction, not a claim about the creators' software.

## Characters
Use slender illustrated figures, narrow outlined limbs, shaped torsos and readable feet. Preserve the proportions of the selected reference family; do not substitute block bodies, thick toy limbs, giant chibi heads, stick figures or realistic anatomy. Build faces with outlined eyes, pupils, brows, nose and mouth shapes. Keep the same face geometry at wide and close scales; a zoom is not a redesign. Distinguish identities through silhouette, build, hair, beard/moustache, complexion, clothing and ornaments. Do not reduce age or body differences to scale alone.

Draw clothing as actual shapes: garment overlap, dhoti/sari folds where relevant, waistband, cloth borders, shoulder drape and hem. Draw necklaces, earrings, arm bands, crowns and other specified ornaments as separate attached components. Use only the selected story's wardrobe; this reference family does not make mythological costumes mandatory for every future story.

## Joints and poses
Construct layered 2D parts with pivots at neck, shoulders, elbows, wrists, hips, knees and ankles. Establish fixed segment lengths. Cover seams with designed overlap rather than conspicuous mechanical circles. Rotate connected parts; do not independently interpolate every joint coordinate if that stretches the limbs. Provide hand replacements for open palm, pointing, grasping and joined palms. Anchor props to the appropriate hand or surface during contact. Separate actor left/right from screen position. Use actual side/back drawings for turns that require them.

Reference windows: R0 34–38 s shows blocking, held confrontation poses and torso tilt; R6 47–51 s shows leaning/recovery and supporting gestures; R9 65–69 s shows sitting, standing and a lifting pose. These examples show limited animation, not proof of physically simulated motion. Choose clear anticipation, action, settle and hold rather than constant bouncing.

## Face and intent
Build neutral, listening, speaking, happy, angry, frightened, sad and surprised states. Coordinate gaze, upper/lower eyelids, brows, mouth and head posture. An O-shaped mouth alone is not enough to distinguish shock from speaking. Preserve identity-specific features through expressions. Use replacement poses and restrained interpolation where appropriate; do not stretch an entire face to simulate emotion. R5 43–47 s illustrates lowered head/sad expression and reaction framing; R9 50–54 s includes a calling gesture.

## Environments
Build base composition first, then recognizable structure, then controlled detail. Palaces/courtyards: arches, columns, railing grids, curtain folds/borders, wall panels, floor divisions and ornaments. Forests: multiple trunk depths, canopy shapes, foreground bushes, ground tufts, paths and isolated props. R2/R5/R6 are useful architectural references; R0/R7/R9 are useful forest references. Preserve layout across cuts. Make detail legible at the intended framing rather than filling every surface with noise. A wide-shot tree can be simpler than the hand-held object in an insert.

## Colour and finishing
Use consistent dark contours and controlled flat fills/illustrated shading. Warm gold, red and earth tones, cooler blue night treatments, gradients, light rays, selective glow, drifting particles, vignette and occasional motion blur are visible in the samples. These are 2D compositing choices; they do not justify photorealistic surfaces or a 3D pipeline. Keep ornaments and faces readable through grading. Do not let effects hide weak drawing or broken poses.

3. Labelled joint pivots, layer order, hand poses and prop-contact anchors.
4. Environment breakdown from silhouette through structural detail to lighting.
5. Short action strips showing anticipation, movement/contact, settle and hold.

Every future sheet must identify its matching code component and supported parameters. Keep a short failure sheet showing incorrect proportions, detached ornaments, stretched arms, sliding feet and excessive realism alongside corrected versions. Do not fabricate measured ratios or exact stroke widths from thumbnails; measure full-size approved drawings when constructing the sheets.
