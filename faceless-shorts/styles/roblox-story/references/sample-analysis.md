## What transfers across stories
- Maintain stable silhouette, hair, clothing and proportions while expressions/poses change.
- Separate character identity from story role and animation. A new protagonist should reuse the same motion engine.
- Build a location kit with furniture, readable background depth and intentional light. Empty plains do not reproduce these references.
- Alternate establishing views, medium acting, face close-ups and prop/device inserts. Use size contrast and scene lighting to signal escalation.
- Use strong, readable face states; allow eye/head/body timing to differ in more advanced clips. Expressive 2D decals are a useful economical technique.
- Synchronize meaningful reveals with narration anchors; keep caption placement adjustable. The samples do not share one universal caption position or highlight color.
- Maintain screen direction and prop ownership across cuts. Reuse locations with changed staging rather than rebuilding a scene for every word.

## What this revision provides and what still needs richer assets
Provides configurable block rigs, basic clothing patterns/hair/accessories, six procedural location kits, eight camera presets, fourteen time-sampled motion routines, held prop sockets, exact token IDs and validated scene plans. Existing renderer lighting/post-processing remains available.

The tall skeletal creature, highly detailed clothing/hair, complex vehicles, richer environments and close-contact choreography in the supplied samples exceed the stock procedural assets. Reaching those shots' detail requires additional original mesh/texture assets and tuned motion. This revision establishes their reusable interface; it does not claim to recreate all five productions at their visual quality.

## Reference acceptance checklist
Watch moving comparisons, not only contact sheets: identity consistency; face readability; cause before reaction; anticipation/contact/settle; grounded feet; furniture contact; coherent eye lines; unobstructed captions; motivated cuts and sound; matching reference lighting density. Tune each component against a short reference-inspired beat, then test it with a different appearance and story. Preserve originality of scripts and characters.
